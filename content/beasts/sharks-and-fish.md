The surface of a lake tells very little of its inhabitants. Loch Modan has its frenzies; the coasts have threshers and sharks large enough to make the view from a boat especially welcome.

These are the fish an angler meets at inconveniently close quarters. A fin in open water is only a fragment of the creature beneath it, and a shoal can turn a quiet pool into sudden movement. The book of life below the surface is a large one; this page covers its more toothsome passages.
