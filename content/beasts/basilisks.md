A basilisk can pass for a stretch of broken rock until it moves. Its low body and thick hide suit the dry hills of Stranglethorn and the southern deserts; the crystals on some older specimens make the resemblance rather convincing.

The gaze is the difficulty. These lizards can leave a living opponent rigid as stone, so the usual naturalist's business of taking a closer look becomes an awkward negotiation. There is much to admire in a basilisk, provided it is looking elsewhere.
