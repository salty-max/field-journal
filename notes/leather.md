# The naturalist's notes on leather and hides

## 2934 Ruined Leather Scraps

Ruined Leather Scraps are small pieces of a skinner's work, too poor to serve as Light Leather on their own.

Leatherworkers can join the scraps into usable Light Leather. A ragged beginning need not be wasted, though the finished piece tells less of the struggle than the original pile did.

## 2318 Light Leather

Light Leather supplies everyday clothing and armour. Veren Tallstrider works with such skins for the people of Thunder Bluff, and offers the pattern for a Kodo Hide Bag in exchange for materials.

Handstitched clothing and Light Armor Kits also use it. A skinner's earliest work can furnish a neighbour as readily as an expedition.

## 2319 Medium Leather

Medium Leather is found on hunts through the Barrens, Hillsbrad and other country beyond the first settlements.

It supplies dark leather clothing and Medium Armor Kits. Smiths also use it in bronze weapons, giving the skinner an interest in the work of the forge.

## 4234 Heavy Leather

Heavy Leather has uses beyond a finished coat. Quartermasters require it for reinforcement and tarpaulins as well as armour, while Master Smith Burninate takes it with coal and Incendosaur Scales for fiery flux.

It also goes into weapons such as the Jade Serpentblade and Moonsteel Broadsword. A hunt can supply the camp, the forge and the swordmaker's bench.

## 4304 Thick Leather

Thick Leather has a place in engineering as well as in clothing. It is called for in supplies for rifle scopes, goggles and steam tanks, and in the reinforcement of zeppelins.

It also goes into mithril armour and weapons. The beasts of Feralas and the Hinterlands can supply a surprising amount of machinery without ever seeing a workshop.

## 8170 Rugged Leather

Rugged Leather can dress more than an armoured traveller. Knot Thimblejack uses it in the ogre suit he makes for visitors to Dire Maul. The disguise is convincing enough for Captain Kromcrush, but only briefly holds together.

The leather also supplies runic clothing and Imperial Plate. Most of those customers prefer their equipment to last longer than an ogre's first impression.

## 15417 Devilsaur Leather

Devilsaur Leather comes from the devilsaurs of Un'Goro Crater. Obtaining the material begins with a rather larger problem than finding room in the bags.

The familiar gauntlets and leggings use it. They are convenient things to admire once the animal involved has stopped objecting.

## 15419 Warbear Leather

The diseased bears of the western Plaguelands and several bears in Winterspring yield Warbear Leather.

It is used for a Warbear Harness and Warbear Woolies. An ordinary bear's leather will not answer those patterns, however fierce the animal looked.

## 15422 Frostsaber Leather

Frostsaber Leather comes from Winterspring's frostsabers. The name is a guide to the animal as well as to the country of the hunt.

Leatherworkers make a set of clothing from it, from boots to tunic. The League still recommends spending a moment observing the living cat before considering its wardrobe.

## 15423 Chimera Leather

Chimeras in Azshara and Winterspring yield this leather.

Chimeric gloves, boots, leggings and vests all use it. The pattern book offers a much quieter introduction to the creatures than a meeting in the field.

## 17012 Core Leather

The core hounds of the Molten Core yield Core Leather. This is work for a skinner travelling with a well-prepared expedition.

It supplies Corehound Boots, the Molten Helm and Core Armor Kits. Such tidy entries in a pattern book give little impression of the animal involved.

## 19767 Primal Bat Leather

Bloodseeker Bats in Zul'Gurub yield Primal Bat Leather. The bats outside the ruins are no substitute for this particular source.

It goes into a primal batskin jerkin, gloves and bracers.

## 19768 Primal Tiger Leather

Zul'Gurub's Zulian cats yield Primal Tiger Leather. Even the expedition's smaller feline encounters deserve a careful record.

Leatherworkers use it for Blood Tiger Breastplates and shoulders. The beasts lend their names to a second set of coats.

## 783 Light Hide

A Light Hide is a raw material that still needs curing, separate from the Light Leather taken on the same hunt. Leatherworkers prepare it with salt before putting it into finer work.

The cured hide goes into Fine Leather Gloves and Embossed Leather Pants. There is a little preparation between the hunt and the stitching.

## 4232 Medium Hide

Medium Hides are cured with salt before being used in further leatherwork. The raw hide and the Medium Leather of the same hunt have different places at the bench.

Cured Medium Hide supplies Dark Leather Gloves and Hillman's Shoulders.

## 4235 Heavy Hide

A Heavy Hide needs curing before it is ready for the patterns that call for it. The process takes more salt than the preparation of the lighter hides.

Once cured, it supplies Guardian Armor and Barbaric Shoulders among other leatherwork. The fiercer names are the leatherworker's contribution, rather than a reliable description of the animal.

## 8169 Thick Hide

Thick Hides are prepared with Deeprock Salt before being put to use. The curing is a different task from sorting the Thick Leather gathered on the same hunt.

The cured hides supply wild leather clothing and the Wolfshead Helm. The resemblance to another creature is the craftsperson's doing.

## 8171 Rugged Hide

Rugged Hides are found on hunts in dangerous country, including Winterspring and Un'Goro Crater.

Curing them calls for Refined Deeprock Salt. Ordinary salt will not do, a detail best learned before arranging an afternoon's work at the bench.

## 249427 Pristine Leather

Daggerfang in Loch Modan, and Lakota'mani and Thunderstomp in the Barrens, can yield Pristine Leather.

Smiths use it in Invulnerable Mail. A find from an early hunting ground can belong to a very ambitious pattern.

## 249428 Pristine Hide

The Maddened Rotclaw in the Wetlands yields Pristine Hide.

Tailors use it in a Bottomless Bag. A hide that helps carry the next expedition's supplies has found a fitting second use.
